## 1. Context and Purpose of the Discussion
A global private equity firm (Speaker 2), which recently invested in an Asian enzyme manufacturer specializing in trypsin for infant formula, is seeking advice from Dr. Dahmer (Speaker 3). Their key client, Nestlé, has requested they build a new factory in Europe to directly supply their European operations, citing geopolitical risk concerns. This new factory would incur higher capital expenditure and labor costs, necessitating a price increase. The company is unfamiliar with the European market and needs guidance on navigating the business dialogue and price negotiations with Nestlé.
## 2. Regulatory and Supplier Selection Hurdles
The discussion addressed the regulatory landscape for using Asian-sourced trypsin in Europe. Key requirements include the enzyme being on the European Commission's registered food enzymes list and the final infant formula receiving approval from the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA), which may require a dossier proving nutritional equivalence. Speaker 3 advised that if the company's trypsin (from pork pancreas) is already registered, its non-European origin is not a barrier, and equivalence can be demonstrated. Speaker 2 stated their belief that the product is registered in Europe.
To navigate the supplier change, Speaker 3 identified four key stakeholder groups at Nestlé beyond procurement: the Patent/IP Department, Factory Operations, and the Regulatory/Safety Department. The factory operations team is particularly important and resistant to changes in established processes ("Don't change a winning team"). The company's current interaction is limited to a single procurement director in Malaysia.
## 3. Recommended Negotiation Strategy with Nestlé
Speaker 3 strongly advised against building a European factory, suggesting it's likely a negotiation tactic from procurement rather than a firm strategic requirement. He noted that Nestlé routinely sources ingredients globally, so shipping enzymes from Asia is not a real barrier. Continuing to negotiate on price alone with the procurement director, who is already demanding a reduction, is considered a losing battle.
The recommended strategy is to reframe the relationship from transactional to a strategic partnership. This involves creating legitimate reasons to engage other key stakeholders at Nestlé, such as research and factory teams. The company should proactively present its R&D, full product portfolio, and a robust backup supply plan to demonstrate value beyond price and enhance its perception as a reliable, high-quality partner. This will also help verify Nestlé's true intentions regarding the European factory. Another proposed tactic is to explore expanding the application of their trypsin to Nestlé's medical nutrition sector, creating new avenues for collaboration.
## 4. Business Development and Future Plans
The company expressed a desire to build a commercial presence in Europe and expand beyond Nestlé. Speaker 3 identified other major infant formula producers like Danone and B2B hydrolysate manufacturers (e.g., Friesland, ARLA, Fonterra) as potential customers. Danone's major hydrolyzed formula factory in the Netherlands was noted as a specific target.
The company, which currently operates as a single-product factory with no European commercial team, will internally discuss the proposed strategies. A follow-up discussion is planned in approximately one week to explore these opportunities further.
