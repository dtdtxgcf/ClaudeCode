## Meeting Notes
### **Analysis of Potential Partners and Business Models**
#### **Martin / Evo Biotics**
*   **Business Model:** Martin's model involves outsourcing the initial crude enzyme extraction to a slaughterhouse and also outsourcing the final "life realization" (lyophilization). This strategy relies on managing margins between two variable components, potentially limiting price flexibility. The speed of their operation is attributed to this outsourcing, which avoids lengthy construction and permitting processes by leveraging existing slaughterhouse permits (e.g., BIMSHI or IPPC).
*   **Company Background:** Evo Biotics is a consulting firm with three equal partners, including Martin. The firm owns an Italian trips site. A significant legal issue was noted: Martin was previously imprisoned for allegedly stealing technology from BioSytica.
*   **Motivation for Partnership:** Evo Biotics seeks a partner for a "trips" extension program to generate more revenue, as they are currently focused on a separate project involving exosomes from eggs, which they see as a bigger opportunity. They are open to a profit-sharing arrangement with a controlling stakeholder.
*   **Risks and Concerns:** The team suspects Martin's group is hiding process details and may be structuring the deal with a management fee to avoid liability. While the low investment cost of three million euros is attractive, it raises suspicion. Additionally, a partner entity, built by the "Natics guy," might be taking regulatory shortcuts, using R&D permits for activities not covered by their registration, such as producing food enzymes like trypsin.
#### **NATEX Alternative**
*   A site visit to NATEX is planned to evaluate building a new facility. NATEX does not have an existing process for "trips," so it would involve recreating the process in a new, smaller hall (approx. 2,000 sq meters). An engineering consulting firm will be hired to ensure an "apple with apple" comparison with other options.
#### **Replicating the Outsourcing Model**
*   The team discussed replicating Martin's outsourcing model. Key challenges include finding a service provider for lyophilization (estimated to be at most five companies) and convincing a slaughterhouse to partner on crude enzyme extraction.
*   It was concluded that outsourcing the crude extraction, which has the largest industrial footprint, to an existing facility could significantly reduce capital expenditure. The subsequent refinement step should not be located at the slaughterhouse.
### **Negotiation Strategy and Customer Relations (Nestlé)**
*   **Customer's Position:** Nestlé is considering new suppliers due to a major quality recall, which has increased the power of their quality department over procurement. The team is one of two options being considered. The team needs to understand the customer's preferences regarding volume, price, and contract duration.
*   **Contract and Price Strategy:** A debate occurred over securing a long-term (five-year) contract with a potentially lower, fixed price versus a shorter (one or two-year) contract with more flexible annual pricing. The team acknowledged being in a strong negotiating position due to Nestlé's limited supply options but also noted a past supply interruption (due to German customs) might be a source of fear of losing the business.
*   **Understanding Customer Motivation:** The team aims to understand the primary driver for Nestlé's interest in a European-made supply. The high engagement from their representative, "Yangyang," suggests a genuine supply issue rather than a negotiation tactic. Potential drivers could be upcoming legislation or new distributor requirements.
*   **Analysis of "Yang Yang" (Nestlé Procurement):** Yangyang is described as ambitious with a fast-track career, which might motivate him to seek a "big win" like a significant price reduction.
### **Internal Assessment and Strategy**
*   **Company's Core Strength and Weakness:** The company's key differentiator is its Ningbo facility, which operates at a European quality standard with a Chinese cost base. However, the company perceives itself as weak in negotiations, possibly due to personality, a desire for a stable relationship with Nestle, or fear stemming from past supply issues. A key issue is the failure to obtain a crucial GMP audit report from Ningbo Linzai, which is a precondition for business with large clients.
*   **Need for Technical Transparency:** The "secret" behind the company's affinity chromatography process is a critical "black box." The team needs to understand if this is a genuine scientific advantage or simply a combination of basic know-how and low-cost manufacturing. There is a need to get a skilled professional to review the company's standing.
*   **Exploring Technology Improvements:** A suggestion was made to explore modern, automated filtration equipment to replace multiple filter presses, which could increase productivity and reduce personnel costs.
*   **Personnel Assessment:** Concerns were raised about the business acumen of "Linda" (the owner's daughter), with a suggestion that she needs more challenging, real-world experience.
*   **Diversification Strategy:** The best way to mitigate dependency on Nestlé is to actively develop business with other customers. One bold strategy mentioned was to temporarily lower prices to eliminate competitors before raising them again.
