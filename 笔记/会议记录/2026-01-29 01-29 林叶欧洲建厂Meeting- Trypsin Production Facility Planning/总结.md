## Meeting Notes
### Raw Material Sourcing and Production Volume
- The target production volume is 600 to 800 tons per year. The final size and cost of the new production line will depend on the target volume (e.g., 10 tons vs. 40 tons of trypsin), which dictates the size of reactors and other equipment.
- Sufficient raw material can be sourced from European countries, with two existing contractors able to handle it. Spain is a large supplier, but engaging them could increase costs due to competitive pricing.
- The team plans to test pancreas from Italian (fatty) and German (good quality but lower activity) sources. Historically, Poland has been the best source, with prices around €3/kg, compared to Spain (€7/kg) and Denmark (cheap but poor quality for pancreatin).
- The European pancreatin market faces a shortage due to decreased pork consumption and high labor costs for harvesting.
### Facility Planning and Construction
- **Land and Building:**
  - 30,000 square meters of land is available for new construction. The new facility will replicate the Shanghai production line but be as small as possible, with an estimated size of around 1,500-2,000 square meters.
  - Building a 1,500-square-meter facility is estimated to take one and a half years, with the Evermont location potentially speeding up the process.
  - Rough cost estimates for a new pharma-grade building vary, cited at €1,500 per square meter, with a past 1,500-square-meter project costing €5-6 million.
- **Existing Facility Use:**
  - The current 10,000-square-meter facility is fully utilized with no empty buildings.
  - Using an existing line is problematic due to cross-contamination risks; an on-site assessment in Italy is required.
  - A new shared GMP warehouse was proposed to free up old warehouse space for laboratory expansion, as the current one is full.
- **Product Grade and Regulatory:**
  - A decision is needed on whether to target GMP/pharma-grade or food-grade production.
  - Food-grade is considered unnecessary, and existing pharma-grade registrations may make it impossible. Pursuing pharma-grade requires higher investment.
  - There is a perceived limited market for a new European pharma-grade product. Trypsin is already registered as an enzyme in Italy, which could offer a faster regulatory path.
### Utility and Equipment Requirements
- **Utilities:**
  - The facility uses municipal, potable water; a Certificate of Analysis (COA) will be provided.
  - The site has a 15,000-volt connection (400 watts peak) that can be extended. 500 megawatts of photovoltaic panels were suggested.
  - Information on electricity, steam, and water consumption from the existing plant is needed to assess capacity for the new plant, as the lyophilization process is a large electricity consumer.
- **Equipment:**
  - Most equipment will be purchased from China, with some items like kegs bought in Italy.
  - A 40-square-meter lyophilizer will be purchased from Don Flon in Italy for an estimated €1.2 million.
### By-product and Wastewater Management
- **Filter Cake:**
  - A key issue is managing filter cake, a residue from pancreatin extraction that contains enzymes and proteins but no residual trypsin. In China, it is sold as animal feed.
  - In Italy, its classification (Category 1, 2, or 3; "waste" vs. "by-product") determines the disposal method (biogas or animal feed) and cost.
  - A standard analysis of the cake's composition is required for classification.
- **Wastewater and Odor:**
  - The primary concern is odor from odorous steam created when by-products from the distillation column are heated.
  - Wastewater is generated at ~30 tons/week, managed by two companies at a cost of ~€50/ton.
  - A proposed solution is a thermocombustor to burn residual solvents and odors for both new and existing facilities. Pancreas gland by-products are classified as Category 3 in Italy.
### Labor Considerations
- Labor costs in Italy are significantly higher than in China. A low-level operator costs approximately €3,500 per month.
- Shorter work shifts in Italy (7 hours vs. 12) mean more staff would be needed for 24-hour operations.
