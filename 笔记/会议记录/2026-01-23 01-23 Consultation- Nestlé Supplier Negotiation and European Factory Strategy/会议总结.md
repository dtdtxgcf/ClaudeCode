# 会议总结

## Key Points
---
- The meeting began with an inquiry about Nestlé's decision-making process for procuring enzymes for hydrolyzed infant formula. The supplier's current contact is limited to research staff and a procurement director in Malaysia.
- It was clarified that while the Malaysian procurement unit exists, the final decision-making authority resides with the global headquarters in Switzerland. The process involves a "top-down and bottom-up reconciliation," where market-level demands are raised and then finalized globally.
- The supplier is being asked by the Malaysian procurement director to establish a factory in Europe due to geopolitical risks. This would increase their costs, and they are unsure how to negotiate the necessary price increase.
- A ballpark figure was provided: building a factory in Europe would likely increase costs by approximately 13% to 15% compared to operating in Asia, assuming all operational, technical, and regulatory deliverables remain constant.
- Building a factory in Europe is unofficially considered important for the supplier's brand positioning and reputation, especially since European-made products are perceived to have a better brand image in some markets.
- The supplier noted that the requirement for a European factory is also driven by regulations mandating that raw materials (animal organs) for infant formula sold in Europe must be sourced and processed within Europe.
- The hierarchy for procurement decisions was clarified: Research & Development (R&D) and Regulatory bodies provide guidance and approval first, and only then does the procurement department act. The expert opinion from R&D is critical and can override pricing considerations.
- Quality is Nestlé's most important factor in procurement, especially after a recent issue. Nestlé is willing to invest up to 10-11% extra for higher quality and more reliable vendors.
- The new CEO's management style has led to more professional processes, enhanced communication between the market and global headquarters, and faster decision-making (e.g., processes that took a month now take two days).
- Nestlé is actively moving away from animal-sourced trypsin towards fermentation-based alternatives for its infant formula. This is considered the future trend and is expected to grow by 32-33%.
- Nestlé's ambition is to have its testing and R&D capabilities for fermentation-based trypsin concluded by the end of 2027.
## Decisions Made
---
- Final procurement decisions for enzymes are made at Nestlé's global headquarters in Switzerland, not at the regional market level.
- Nestlé's procurement process is guided first by Research & Development and Regulatory approvals. Procurement does not act without their sign-off, especially for sensitive products like infant formula.
- Quality is prioritized over price. Nestlé will invest more in reliable suppliers with high-quality products.
