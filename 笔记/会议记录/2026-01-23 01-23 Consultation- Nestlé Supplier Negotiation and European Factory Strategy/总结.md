## Background
The client is an existing Asian supplier of enzymes to Nestlé. Their current interactions are limited to research personnel and a procurement director in Malaysia. The core of the discussion revolves around a request from this procurement director for the client to establish a manufacturing facility in Europe. This request is driven by geopolitical risk concerns and potential European regulations requiring local sourcing for raw materials (animal organs) used in enzymes for infant formula sold in the EU. The client is concerned about the high capital expenditure and operational costs of a European factory, which would necessitate a significant price increase. They are seeking to understand the internal dynamics and decision-making process at Nestlé to formulate an effective negotiation strategy.
## Pain Points
The client faces a strategic dilemma due to a request from a Nestlé procurement director to build a factory in Europe. This creates a major cost and negotiation challenge, as their current competitive advantage is lower pricing from their Asian base. If they build a European factory, they estimate their costs will increase by **13% to 14%**, and potentially up to **15%**. They are uncertain how to justify this price hike to Nestlé and what negotiation strategy to employ with the procurement director. The core problem is the fear that failing to build the factory could result in losing Nestlé's business to European competitors, while building it erodes their price competitiveness and requires significant capital investment. They lack clarity on whether this request is a non-negotiable, company-wide mandate from Nestlé's global headquarters or a preference of the regional procurement team.
The client has limited visibility into Nestlé's complex, multi-layered decision-making process for enzyme procurement. They are currently only in contact with research staff and a **procurement director based in Malaysia**, but the consultant clarified that final decision-making power resides with the **global headquarters in Switzerland**. The client is unsure who the key stakeholders are and how to influence them effectively, including the relative influence of R&D experts versus the procurement director. This uncertainty hinders their ability to verify the true importance of building a European factory and to effectively promote their products. The hierarchy was described as starting with **R&D**, followed by **Regulatory**, and then **Procurement**, with a Business Development Advisor ensuring commercial viability.
