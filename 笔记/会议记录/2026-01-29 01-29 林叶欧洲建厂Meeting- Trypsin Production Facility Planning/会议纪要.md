[01:45-03:12] **Wastewater management is a major concern, with odor being the primary challenge despite having an effective disposal system.**
- A production target of 600 to 800 tons per year is confirmed as acceptable.
- Wastewater treatment is identified as a primary concern for the inquiring party.
- The current system involves two service companies that collect approximately 30 tons of wastewater and 5 tons of by-products (water from glands and fat) weekly.
- The main problem is not the treatment itself but the odor generated.
- The odor is created because by-products from the distillation column are at 90°C and contain fat, creating an odorous steam. This odor treatment must be handled at the biogas plant where the material is sent.
[03:12-04:46] **A new facility would include a thermocombustor to solve odor issues, and the current by-product disposal is cost-effective and not a bottleneck.**
- The biogas plants receiving the wastewater are requesting more material than is currently produced, indicating no capacity bottleneck.
- For a new facility, the proposed solution for odor is a thermocombustor to burn residual solvents and odors, which would serve both the existing and new operations.
- This investment would help meet increasingly strict environmental authority specifications and resolve issues with public odor complaints.
- The current cost for wastewater disposal is approximately €50 per ton, and the service providers handle the treatment at their plants near Milan.
- Key Decision: For a future new facility, a thermocombustor will be installed to manage and eliminate odors from both operations.
[04:46-05:15] **By-products from pancreas glands are classified as Category 3 in Italy, and the disposal process for filter cake residue is questioned.**

[13:15-16:10] **The Trypsin filter cake could potentially be managed as a Category 3 by-product, but this requires a formal process and lab analysis.**
- The alternative is to classify the cake as a Category 3 by-product, similar to current waste streams. It could be handled in either solid or liquid form.
- To classify the product, a complete process description is required to identify where the by-product is generated.
- A lab must then analyze a sample against a full list of tests to determine its classification:
    - **Category 1:** Must be destroyed.
    - **Category 2:** Can be used for animal feed.
    - **Category 3:** Can be used in a by-products market (e.g., biogas production).
- The cost of disposal is tied to this classification. The current rate for Category 3 material is ~€50/ton, and this is expected to be similar for the cake.
- The only logistical change would be handling a solid (in big bags) instead of the current liquid which is pumped into a tanker truck.
- Action Item: @Ms. Yeh - Share the process steps where the by-product/waste is created so it can be sent for analysis.

- This is identified as a potential issue for biogas plants, as nitrogen compounds (NH2, NH3) are difficult to digest and are the primary source of odor.
