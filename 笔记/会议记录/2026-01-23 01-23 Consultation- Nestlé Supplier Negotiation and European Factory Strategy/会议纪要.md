## Detailed Minutes
[00:11-02:57] **Nestlé's procurement decision-making for enzymes is centralized at its Swiss global headquarters, despite input from local markets.**
- The process involves a "top-down and bottom-up reconciliation," where local markets, such as the one in Malaysia, identify and raise demands for specific enzyme combinations (e.g., dual enzyme systems like trypsin-like endopeptidase), source materials (e.g., non-animal derived chymosin), and process codes.
- These market-level requirements are sent to the global headquarters in Switzerland.
- The global headquarters scouts for potential suppliers and consults back with the local market.
- **Key Decision:** The final supplier selection decision rests solely with the global headquarters in Switzerland, not the local procurement units. - This structure applies even to detailed specifications like enzyme type (e.g., chymosin-like serine endopeptidase).
[03:01-03:27] **Nestlé's global headquarters is responsible for the risk assessment of microbial enzymes.**
- This responsibility includes conducting toxicological and allergenicity assessments.
- An example of this assessment is the comparison of hydrolysis results.
[03:28-06:13] **A supplier facing a request to build a European factory due to geopolitical risk can expect a significant price increase of 13-15% compared to Asian production.**
- The supplier, currently based in Asia, has a competitive price but was asked by the Nestlé procurement director in Malaysia to establish a factory in Europe to mitigate geopolitical risks.
- The supplier is concerned that the higher capital expenditure (capex) and labor costs in Europe will necessitate a price increase, and they are unsure how to negotiate this.
- Speaker 1 provided a "ballpark" estimate that a European price point would be approximately 13% to 14% higher than an Asian one (e.g., $100 in Asia would be $113-$114 in Europe).
- This price increase assumes all other deliverables remain constant, including:
    - **Operational Deliverables:** Supplier evaluation scorecards, risk assessments, regulatory readiness, go-to-market plans, and pricing/margin models.
    - **Technical Deliverables:** Hydrolysis process recommendations, enzyme selection frameworks, and allergenicity risk models.
[06:13-07:09] **Nestlé utilizes internal tools and processes, like the "ARA DHA supplier risk concord" (EAA tool), to evaluate supplier switching costs and align procurement with R&D strategy.**
- This tool is used to assess factors such as long-term supply scenarios and the costs associated with switching suppliers.
- Local/national procurement teams provide strategic input to the Research & Development (R&D) department.
- These inputs inform R&D on specifications like the required degree of hydrolysis, enzyme system, and target peptide size.
- The process also incorporates crisis and risk management planning.
[07:15-11:14] **Building a factory in Europe is considered important for both regulatory compliance and brand perception when supplying Nestlé.**
- The supplier is seeking to validate if the Malaysian procurement director's request for a European factory reflects Nestlé's overall corporate intention, fearing they might lose orders to European newcomers if they fail to comply.
- Speaker 1 unofficially confirmed that a lack of a manufacturing footprint in Europe negatively impacts a supplier's brand positioning and reputation for supplying the European market.
- Conversely, products manufactured in Europe (e.g., at Nestlé's Germany facility) are perceived by consumers in Asian and Middle Eastern markets as having a better brand image.
- The supplier clarified their primary driver for a European factory is regulation, not branding. European regulations require that if a final infant formula is sold in Europe, its raw materials (in this case, animal organs for enzyme extraction) must also be sourced and processed within Europe.
- Speaker 1 acknowledged the regulatory requirement but reiterated the significant positive impact on consumer perception and brand image for European-made products, citing his experience with Nestlé Health Science products.
[11:21-14:16] **At Nestlé, technical experts and R&D have definitive authority over procurement decisions for specialized ingredients like hydrolyzed proteins.**
- The procurement department is guided by experts; if an expert advises against a purchase, procurement will not proceed, regardless of price.
- The hierarchical flow of influence is:
    1.  Research and Development (R&D)
    2.  Regulatory
    3.  Procurement
- A "Business Development Advisor" role ensures commercial viability (market entry, pricing, positioning) after the technical and regulatory checks are complete, before final review by executive leadership.
- **Key Decision:** For infant feed and hydrolyzed protein systems, Nestlé's procurement will not "move an inch" without guidance and approval from R&D, technical, and regulatory teams.
[14:16-16:21] **Quality, as signed off by R&D and Regulatory, is the most important factor in Nestlé's enzyme procurement process, preceding any commercial action.**
- The primary input for procurement decisions is from R&D.
- R&D must first sign off on all quality-related elements, including the quality of enzymes, hydrolysis formulation, and overall quality assurance.
- Following R&D approval, the product must secure regulatory approvals (e.g., EFSA, FDA, Codex, Halal, China's SAMR). Nestlé is very serious about these audits.
- Only after both R&D sign-off and regulatory approvals are complete does the procurement department begin its work.
[16:22-18:08] **In response to quality issues like the "ARA issue," Nestlé prioritizes quality and reliability over price, and is willing to invest more to ensure it.**
- Recent quality issues have reinforced Nestlé's focus on material quality.
- The company will choose quality even if it costs more, as extensive checks, audits, and taste profiling are in place.
- **Key Decision:** Nestlé is willing to invest up to 10-11% extra to improve quality or to select vendors who are proven to be more reliable.
[18:10-20:20] **Under the new CEO, Nestlé is becoming more professional and efficient, with improved communication and faster supplier approvals.**
- The company is evolving its systems, processes, and approach to technologies like AI in the supply chain.
- A key improvement is enhanced communication between local markets and global headquarters, which has improved reconciliation and supplier approvals.
- Processes that previously took a month can now be completed in as little as two days.
- This efficiency is supported by systems and planning methodologies, specifically the "Advanced Planning Optimizer" (APO).
- APO enables integrated planning across demand, distribution, master/detailed production scheduling, and Material Requirements Planning (MRP), which is where enzyme procurement occurs.

[21:11-23:44] **Nestlé is strategically shifting from animal-sourced trypsin to fermentation-based trypsin for infant formula, targeting completion by the end of 2027.**
- The current standard for trypsin is animal-sourced (e.g., from porcine pancreas), but the clear future trend is toward fermentation-based alternatives.
- Speaker 1 noted that Nestlé is moving away from animal-sourced materials as a global direction.
- The market space for fermentation-based production is projected to increase by 32-33%.
- This shift requires a significant amount of R&D and QA-related risk assessment.
- **Key Decision:** Nestlé's ambition is to transition to fermentation-based trypsin by the end of 2027, by which time testing and R&D capabilities are expected to be concluded.
