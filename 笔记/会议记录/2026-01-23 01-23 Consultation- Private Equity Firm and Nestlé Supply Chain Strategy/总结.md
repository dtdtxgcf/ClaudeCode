## Overview
The consultation is with a global private equity firm that recently acquired a specialized Asia-based enzyme manufacturer. This manufacturer is a long-standing, passive supplier of trypsin to Nestlé's infant formula division. The client's primary challenge stems from pressure from Nestlé's procurement director in Malaysia to both reduce their offering price and build a new, costly factory in Europe to supply European operations directly, citing geopolitical risks. This creates a financial conflict, as higher European production costs would necessitate a price increase. The client, a PE firm aiming to create value, is hesitant due to the high costs, lack of a European presence, and their product's current significant price advantage. Their interactions are confined to a single procurement contact, limiting their understanding of Nestlé's broader priorities and whether the European factory is a genuine strategic imperative or a negotiation tactic. The client seeks advice on navigating this complex relationship with Nestlé, validating the need for the factory, gaining access to other key stakeholders (R&D, factory managers), and exploring business development opportunities with other major producers like Danone.
## Background
The client is a private equity firm that acquired a specialized trypsin manufacturing company in the last year. This portfolio company is described as a "factory" rather than a commercial entity, focused on producing high-quality **trypsin derived from pork pancreas**. For the past five to six years, they have been a strategic supplier to major infant formula producers like **Nestlé and Danone**. The relationship with Nestlé, initiated by Nestlé due to a potential supply shortage, is managed entirely through a **procurement team based in Malaysia**. They currently supply trypsin from their Asian factory to a Nestlé factory in Europe; however, this trypsin is only used for producing formula exported **outside of Europe**. For formula sold within Europe, Nestlé uses a different, local European supplier. Nestlé has now requested the client's company to build a new factory in Europe to supply the European market directly. This presents a challenge as the client has a significant price advantage (about **half the price** of a Canadian competitor), and the **higher capex and labor costs in Europe** would force a price increase. The client lacks a commercial or R&D presence in Europe and is unfamiliar with its specific regulations and Nestlé's internal decision-making structure.
## Pain Points
The client's primary pain point is the **uncertainty and risk associated with expanding into the European market at Nestlé's request**. They face significant pressure from Nestlé's procurement director to **reduce their offering price** while simultaneously being pushed to **build a costly new factory in Europe**. This creates a direct financial conflict, as the investment would raise their cost base, making a price reduction unfeasible. The pressure for a price cut is driven by the procurement director citing **newcomers in the market**, while the push for a European factory is framed as a response to **geopolitical concerns**. This dual pressure puts the client in a difficult negotiating position, as they feel they lack the bargaining power and fear they might **"lose this client"** within five years if they don't comply, but complying is financially challenging and may not be necessary. They are hesitant to invest heavily in a European factory without a clear strategic justification, especially since they have **no experience or personnel** to manage such a project.
A second major pain point is the **lack of access and visibility into Nestlé’s internal decision-making process**. Their interaction is confined to a single procurement director in Malaysia whose focus is solely on **price and transactional details**. This prevents them from engaging with other critical stakeholders like **factory managers, operations, R&D, and regulatory teams**. The client cannot validate if the demand for a European factory is a company-wide strategic imperative or just a negotiating tactic from procurement. They suspect that others within Nestlé may prefer them to **continue supplying from Asia** due to the complexities of qualifying a new facility.
This lack of access also means they do not understand why Nestlé uses a different trypsin supplier for its European-market products. They fear there may be a hidden regulatory barrier, a technical incompatibility, or a long-standing preference for the current supplier. The consultant suggested this could be because the existing supplier's enzyme was used in the original regulatory filing with the **European Food Safety Authority (EFSA)** and is on the **positive list of the register for food enzymes**, a validation process Nestlé may have gone through with that supplier over the past **thirty years**. This uncertainty prevents them from accurately assessing the feasibility of becoming the primary European supplier.
Finally, the company operates in a passive, reactive commercial mode, essentially **"waiting for the client to come to us."** With a single-product focus, they lack the commercial arm or strategy to pursue business development opportunities with other major companies like **Danone and Mead Johnson**, leaving potential revenue on the table.
## Expectations
The client expects to gain a clear understanding of **how to approach the business negotiation with Nestlé**. They seek actionable strategies to handle the conflicting demands on price and the new factory, moving the conversation beyond a purely transactional one. A primary goal is to **validate whether building a factory in Europe is a genuine and firm requirement from Nestlé as a whole**, to avoid a potentially disastrous investment. Success would be having a definitive answer to the question: **"whether Nestle really want us to come to Europe."**
The client is also seeking effective methods to **reach out to and gain insights from other key stakeholders within Nestlé**, such as factory managers and R&D teams, bypassing the procurement gatekeeper without damaging the relationship. The objective is to establish direct lines of communication to understand true operational needs, regulatory hurdles, and the real drivers behind the European factory request.
Furthermore, the client wants to **receive clear guidance on European regulatory requirements** to confirm their trypsin is compliant for use in infant formula sold within the EU. They need to understand the process for getting their enzyme approved and demonstrating equivalence to the one currently in use.
Finally, the client wants to establish a strategy for **proactive business development in Europe**, particularly with other major producers like **Danone**. They expect advice on how to approach these companies, identify key contacts and manufacturing sites (e.g., Danone's factory in the **Netherlands**), and understand their supply chain dynamics, thereby creating a roadmap for expansion.

- The specific Nestlé factory for HA formulas is in Biesenhofen, Germany; the associated research center is in Konolfingen, Switzerland.
