## Meeting Notes
- Topic Title: Company Overview and Strategic Focus
  - Description:
    - Evobiotics focuses on evolution-inspired biologic therapeutics, developing IP and proprietary extraction methods from natural raw materials (milk, eggs, feathers) and supplying actives to partners who bring products to market.
    - Lean team enables rapid decisions; core roles include [Speaker 1] (customers/R&D), Raphael (regulatory), and a production lead.
    - Founded in Switzerland (2017); HQ in Switzerland, exosome technical center in Austria, production primarily in Northern Italy; leveraging proximity for audits and oversight.
    - Transitioning from R&D investment to scaling; long-term aspiration includes profitability and potential turnover around 10 million.
    - Strategic focus prioritizes larger, scalable opportunities (e.g., milk exosomes, selected proteins) over smaller, crowded markets like trypsin/pancreatic enzymes.
  - Conclusion:
    - Positioned as a nimble, IP-driven operator focused on scalable biologics, with a European footprint and partner-driven commercialization.
- Topic Title: Product Portfolio and Market Context
  - Description:
    - Lysozyme: legacy OTC respiratory API with GMP-certified production via JV in Lanzhou; DMFs in multiple Asian markets; strong demand in Japan (~100 MT) vs lower in China (~5 MT).
    - Transferrin (egg-derived): analogous to lactoferrin; used in Korea for iron-binding supplements.
    - New R&D: milk-derived exosomes for drug delivery, APIs, and cosmetics; active keratin from feathers (biologically active), developed with a German university.
    - Exosomes patent claims enable industrial-scale production; many other methods yield only grams and are costly; initial trial customers show traction.
    - Egg raw material dynamics: industrial plants break 10–15 million eggs/day; SPF eggs and vaccine sector cause seasonal volatility; Japan large market for egg white proteins.
  - Conclusion:
    - Strong Asia-focused legacy APIs and emerging protein/exosome lines; exosomes and keratin are near-term cosmetic opportunities with longer pharma horizons.
- Topic Title: Manufacturing Model, Partnerships, and Economics
  - Description:
    - Hybrid “open-book” model: Evobiotics controls process, machinery, certifications, and customer contracts; places equipment on partner premises that provide location and raw materials; staffing on partner payroll for compliance; Evobiotics manages quality for its processes.
    - Structures vary by project: cost-plus predominates, with Evobiotics retaining marketing margin; some profit-sharing exists; not pure outsourcing.
    - Rationale: colocate near raw materials to avoid transporting bulk inputs and mitigate supply risk; applied across 3–4 products (milk, egg proteins, etc.).
    - Partner economics: transforms low-margin (3–4%) operations into high-margin (50–60%) niches; efficiency gains (staffing ~one-third of peers).
  - Conclusion:
    - A JV-style, cost-plus, on-premises equipment model aligns incentives, reduces overhead, and maintains control and compliance while leveraging partner infrastructure.
- Topic Title: Regulatory, Permitting, and Traceability
  - Description:
    - Using established partner facilities eases greenfield challenges but requires new permits and detailed process descriptions; regional regimes differ.
    - European production emphasizes traceability and full-chain food-grade assurance; response time <1 hour vs ~24 hours for others; closed supply chains prioritized to avoid contaminant concentration risks.
    - Logistics to the US face customs delays and duties; cross-border raw material movement (e.g., EU to Canada) complicates compliance.
    - For new sites (e.g., Austria), conversion to compliant manufacturing must align with EU 852/853; verification of utilities (water, wastewater, freezing, HVAC) and municipal treatment connections required.
  - Conclusion:
    - Regulatory planning and location strategy are integral; EU-based, traceable, food-grade production is a key differentiator for large customers.
- Topic Title: Market Landscape for Pancreatic Enzymes/Trypsin
  - Description:
    - Market size estimated ~20–35 MT across applications; Nestlé-related volumes ~9–10 MT; dominated by a few suppliers (e.g., Nihon Technology); customers seek alternatives due to reliability and costly, inefficient logistics (transcontinental shipping, freeze-drying).
    - Competitor signals: Canadian player invested ~€2M in freeze-dryer; moving production to Europe needs significant capex; Enzimas likely maintains 3–5 MT backup capacity; margins improved via pricing and spot procurement.
    - Raw material (pancreas) availability varies by region; northern European constraints and disease risks (e.g., ASF) affect supply; careful scaling and sourcing needed to manage price/supply risk.
  - Conclusion:
    - Demand exists for traceable, efficient European supply; Evobiotics remains pragmatic about investment and phased capacity (5–10 MT) while monitoring competitor moves and supply constraints.
- Topic Title: Scale-Up Strategy, Capacity, and Site Planning
  - Description:
    - Phased approach: start ~5 MT with buffers, target up to 10 MT; avoid overcommitment and long-term debt; maintain flexibility to align with market/supply realities.
    - Austria site advantages: existing cold-chain (-23°C and 0–4°C), cross-docking, large hub within ~100 km; potential to store hundreds of tons and supply weekly needs; owner operates largest groupage cold-chain network in Austria.
    - Facility scope: integrate manufacturing into existing site (not replacing storage); initial operational area ~1,000 m² for phase one; high ceilings (~8 m) but no need for tall tanks; process designed as a copy of an existing setup with incremental improvements.
    - Freeze-drying excluded from in-house scope; outsourced to a European subcontractor using a patented energy-efficient system; final packing at Aujin clean room.
    - Cost estimates: hall refurbishment ~0.5M leveraging existing structure/sewage; equipment sized toward 10 MT; current figures include equipment and installation but exclude engineering consultancy, permits, ground works, and freeze-drying investment; COVID-era experience added ~20% cost due to steel/PLC shortages.
  - Conclusion:
    - Begin with modest footprint and capacity in Austria leveraging existing cold-chain; replicate proven processes, outsource lyophilization, and refine comprehensive CAPEX/OPEX before committing.
- Topic Title: Partnership Models, Governance, and Antitrust
  - Description:
    - Structures vary: cost-plus, some profit-sharing, JV equity preferred for long-term alignment; management-fee-only arrangements are not desired; concern about technology contribution without sustained benefits or dilution of control.
    - Discussions with Linz/Linzhine and Hillhouse: exploring collaboration feasibility; requests for clarity on control (majority/minority), governance, and economic models; term-sheet options and staged information sharing proposed.
    - Due diligence vs information sensitivity: small company seeks to protect competitive data; suggests NDA plus staged data room; Hillhouse requires diligence-heavy approach; facility info sharing to follow relationship framework.
    - Antitrust sensitivities flagged in a concentrated market; requires legal scrutiny; not a blocker at current stage.
    - Hillhouse introduction: >$100B AUM, global footprint, strong healthcare focus (~30% capital), significant BeiGene stake; interest to move forward pending investment committee approval, requiring diligence and forecasts.
  - Conclusion:
    - Collaboration will be proposal-driven with staged diligence under NDA, legal review of antitrust, and preference for long-term aligned JV structures over consultancy or distributor-led models.
- Topic Title: Economics, Freeze-Drying Costs, and Energy Sensitivity
  - Description:
    - Energy volatility affects operations; for enzymes, water/wastewater costs are critical; gas related; freeze-drying is energy-intensive.
    - Lyophilization partners claim 10–15% of typical energy usage; service costs often quoted per kg of ice input (€8.5–10.5/kg), translating to ~€50–80/kg of final product depending on solids; spray drying is cheaper but unsuitable for certain products.
    - A €20–50/kg difference materially impacts margins; cost modeling should use input basis and dry matter assumptions; utilities context affects EBITDA variance.
  - Conclusion:
    - Adopt standardized freeze-drying cost model based on input mass and solids; evaluate energy-efficient partners to improve margins and quantify utility-driven EBITDA sensitivity.
- Topic Title: Decision Process, Investment Number, and Risk Sharing
  - Description:
    - Need for a well-substantiated investment figure (e.g., “2.5” discussed) with assumptions, timeline, and scope; internal review required before committing.
    - Lease terms for proposed site could be 3–5 years with buy option; ensure future-proofing.
    - Investors requested commitment to funding and 18-month timeline; proponents willing to proceed but need supplier verification, detailed engineering, and internal discussions (e.g., with Mark).
    - Investment structure options: management fee vs equity/royalty tied to EBITDA over set duration; Evobiotics prefers aligned long-term participation; investor side to send concrete proposal with parameters (royalty rate/duration or EBITDA-linked).
    - Governance: 66% of the company represented with unanimous decisions; remaining ~32% not present; avoid short-notice meetings; stage proposals (3–5 options) and proceed step by step.
  - Conclusion:
    - A concrete, proposal-driven pathway is required to define investment amount, timeline, risk-sharing, governance, and structure before commitments.
- Topic Title: Market Demand, Distributor Concerns, and Prioritization
  - Description:
    - Customers (e.g., Nestlé) prefer direct, traceable European supply; dissatisfaction with costly intermediary structures and opaque distributors capturing margin.
    - Historical relationships with Canadian suppliers acknowledged; concerns about transparency and concurrent work with competitors (e.g., Merck activity in Italy).
    - Prioritization under scenarios where Canada supplies ~5 MT and Europe ~5 MT: Evobiotics confident in product/price, designs operations to scale up/down, avoids relying on few customers, and aims to compete sustainably rather than fight capital-heavy rivals.
  - Conclusion:
    - Strategy targets direct, transparent, EU-compliant supply and flexible scaling, reducing reliance on intermediaries and single large customers.
