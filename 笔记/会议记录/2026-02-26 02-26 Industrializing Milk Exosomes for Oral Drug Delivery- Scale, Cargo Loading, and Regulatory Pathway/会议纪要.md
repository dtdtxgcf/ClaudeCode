[00:24-00:57] **The organization's strategy involves participation in small companies to understand local business environments and identify optimal locations for establishing new entities.**

[00:57-02:16] **An overview of milk exosome research was presented as an example of the organization's advanced research capabilities, highlighting its potential as a next-generation drug delivery system.**
- The speaker transitions from robotics to present research on milk exosomes, noting its high relevance to the pharmaceutical industry and pathology.
- Exosomes are described as a "hot" research topic, with UK startup EVOKS Therapeutics mentioned as an example.
- Discovered roughly 10-15 years ago, exosomes hold the promise of being a next-generation drug delivery system.
[02:16-04:07] **Exosomes are complex, cell-produced nanoparticles capable of efficiently entering cells, similar to viruses, making them a fascinating but challenging subject of study.**
- The ultimate goal is to use exosomes to deliver complex molecules like proteins orally, eliminating the need for injections.
- Exosomes are structurally more complex than proteins, consisting of a cell membrane fragment enclosing contents like proteins, microRNAs, nucleic acids, and enzymes.
- Electron microscopy images show a crowded interior and a surrounding membrane.
- Exosomes share an uptake mechanism with viruses, allowing them to enter cells very efficiently, which is a key reason for their research interest.
- A major challenge is their production, as they constitute a very small fraction of the total mass in traditional cell cultures, making it difficult to produce sufficient quantities.
[04:27-05:19] **The research group has overcome production challenges by isolating exosomes from milk, enabling large-scale production, but now faces the challenge of loading drugs into these natural exosomes.**
- The group can produce kilograms of milk exosomes, a significant advantage over typical cell culture methods which yield only a few milliliters.
- Researchers using cell culture can use genetic modification and molecular biology to incorporate drugs during the exosome's formation.
- The group's challenge is different: they must develop methods to load drugs into the pre-existing, naturally sourced milk exosomes post-isolation.
[05:19-07:12] **The organization has established a patented industrial production process for milk exosomes and is actively engaged in collaborations and further patent development.**
- Patents for the industrial production of milk exosomes have been granted in Italy and Switzerland and are at the PCT (Patent Cooperation Treaty) stage.
- A pilot production plant has been established to produce research-grade material in a controlled manner.
- **Key Collaboration:** The group is collaborating with Roche in China, providing milk exosomes for their research into peptide delivery and GLP production.
- A second patent was filed at the end of 2025 for a method to load RNAs into exosomes.
- High-resolution fluorescence imaging has successfully visualized RNA (green) inside the organelle, distinct from the milk proteins (red).
- The platform offers numerous research opportunities for junior researchers.
[07:12-08:47] **Financing for the exosome research was primarily generated through a collaboration with Boehringer Ingelheim, and the project has progressed through process verification, animal studies, and biodistribution analysis since 2020.**
- A collaboration with Boehringer Ingelheim, a major German pharmaceutical company, provided the funding for the exosome research.
- The project timeline began in 2020 with process verification for large-scale production and cell study validation.
- Animal studies (in mice) were conducted to determine the biodistribution of orally administered, fluorescently-labeled milk exosomes, identifying which organs they reached.
- Histology of a mouse liver shows the distribution of the exosomes within the organ.
- The group also worked on surface-displaying GFP ligands for a company in China and used their "lipomix" platform to deliver non-proprietary drugs to different tissues.
[08:49-09:15] **The intellectual property for the exosome technology is shared between the company and the university.**
- The technology was initially developed jointly with a university.
- Key Decision: The company took ownership of the IP because the university lacked funds for maintenance. The current ownership split is 80% for the company and 20% for the university, and they continue to collaborate.
[09:16-10:52] **The company primarily relies on corporate funding and collaborations rather than grants, which are considered inefficient and reserved for highly speculative projects.**
- The exosome project was funded by a company, not a grant.
- While the company does apply for grants sometimes (three in the last three years), they are considered slow and inefficient.
- The production process can yield extracts of varying purity levels through methods like size exclusion chromatography:
    - **High Purity (90%):** A milk immunoglobulin preparation.
    - **Medium Purity:** Contains mainly lactoferrin and immunoglobulins.
    - **Crude Preparation:** A whey protein extract used in cosmetics, containing good quantities of immunoglobulins and lactoferrin.
- The material is characterized using an IR instrument to quantify lipids, protein, and phosphates, and to analyze the secondary structure of proteins for quality control fingerprinting.
[10:53-12:46] **Comprehensive characterization and stability studies confirm the identity, purity, and activity of the milk exosomes, with research showing they can cross the blood-brain barrier.**
- Characterization methods include nanoparticle analysis (NPA) to count particles, chromatography, Western blotting, and enzymatic assays for membrane proteins.
- Imaging confirms that the exosomes are taken up into cells.
- Stability studies compared liquid, frozen-thawed, and other storage conditions (4°C, -80°C) to assess how well they retain activity over time.
- A key finding from biodistribution studies is that the exosomes can get into the brain, a significant discovery as this is typically very difficult to achieve.
- The presence of the delivered material in the brain was observed at 12, 36, 60, and 108 hours.
- Liver imaging shows that nearly every cell had taken up the material.
[13:16-15:12] **The core technology for milk exosome production stems from a 1995 patent for a process that pasteurizes egg whites at high temperatures without coagulation.**
- The company developed and patented a process in 1995 that allows egg whites to be pasteurized at 120-130°C without coagulating.
- This patent, which was sold to an egg producer, enabled the development of shelf-stable, room-temperature drinks.
- The same underlying technology from this patent allowed for the production of milk exosomes.
- The group has published its findings in various journals, with articles cited from 2020 through July 2023, including a collaboration with the Giannini Pharma company on a product for rare disorders.
[15:13-17:20] **The operational management of the production plants involves a hybrid model of remote monitoring and on-site supervision, with dedicated staff and flexible time allocation based on need.**
